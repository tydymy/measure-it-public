> **What this is and is not.** These tables show which objective measurements researchers **register in trials** and **describe in NIH-funded projects** for each condition. A trial using a measurement is not evidence that the measurement works or detects the condition. An FDA record is a deployment-readiness signal, not evidence that a device diagnoses the target illness. All counts come from pattern text mining, and an **AI reviewer (a language model, not a domain expert)** audited its precision snippet by snippet.

## 2. Negative, null and cautionary results

1. **The shipped patterns were imprecise for most classes.** In round 1, 15 of 26 classes had strict precision below 0.8. The failure modes were out-of-scope uses (resting 12-lead ECG counted as ambulatory ECG, clinic spot-check SpO2 and vital-sign respiratory rate, stopwatch sit-to-stand counted as digital gait), homonyms ('Mast Cell Activation' matching the T-cell pattern, 'Oct 2019', 'Valutazione' matching zio, FMD = functional movement disorder, cellular 'respiration rate'), background sentences in grant abstracts, and animal work.
2. **After one revision, 3 class(es) still fail the 0.8 precision bar on a fresh sample:** `immune_assays` 0.67 (0.49-0.81) (n=30, seeded_random_30); `posture_detection` 0.00 (0.00-0.32) (n=8, census); `transcriptomics` 0.57 (0.39-0.73) (n=30, seeded_random_30). Their counts contain many false matches and are flagged (`text_mining_precision_below_0_8`, `text_mining_precision_status`) in the registry and in every query result. For `posture_detection` the revision made things worse: a new 'time spent sitting/standing' phrase pulled in IPAQ questionnaire items and standing-tolerance tests. Of the 15 revised classes, only 6 meet the bar on an adequate fresh sample (>= 10 judged mentions from >= 5 distinct records). **6 class(es) have a point estimate >= 0.8 on too small a sample to say they pass:** `ppg` 1.00 from 10 mention(s) in 4 record(s); `respiratory_rate` 1.00 from 1 mention(s) in 1 record(s); `small_fiber_testing` 1.00 from 2 mention(s) in 2 record(s); `capillaroscopy` 1.00 from 10 mention(s) in 4 record(s); `digital_gait` 0.83 from 6 mention(s) in 4 record(s); `remote_patient_monitoring` 0.88 from 8 mention(s) in 5 record(s).
3. **`continuous_temperature` could not be re-audited on fresh data:** every post-revision demo-cluster mention had already been judged in the round-1 census. Its round-1 precision on the original patterns was 0.70 (0.49-0.84). Re-scored on the judged mentions that survive the revision, it is 0.83 (n=18), which is design data and therefore optimistic.
4. **Judged mentions cluster within records, so mention-level intervals are too narrow.** Several mentions often come from one trial (e.g. 8 of the 30 round-2 `vascular_imaging` mentions are one trial; the 30 round-1 `retinal_imaging` mentions come from 9 records). The table in section 3 adds a conservative record-clustered interval (worst-case effective sample size). Precision is also measured per mention, whereas the counts are per trial, and only on demo-cluster records: precision in the other ten conditions is not measured.
5. **Dysautonomia counts are dominated by a broad condition term.** 144 of the 298 literal-match dysautonomia trials link only through 'autonomic dysfunction', mostly autonomic dysfunction secondary to diabetes, chronic kidney disease, heart failure, sepsis, anaesthesia or Parkinson disease. Trials that measure HRV often call their outcome 'cardiac autonomic dysfunction', so this link is partly defined by the measurement itself. Through the specific terms only, HRV falls from 88 to 27 trials and from 31 to 4 NIH core projects; autonomic testing from 88 to 61; the wearable bundle from 103 to 35 trials. The `*_specific_terms` columns of `condition_measurement_evidence` carry this view.
6. **The 'wearable' bundle is partly not wearable.** Its HRV member is device-agnostic (laboratory, tilt-table, Holter or wearable). Across the 14 conditions the bundle has 374 trials, 195 without HRV. The precision audit judged HRV against 'heart-rate variability' in any setting, so its 0.93 does not say the HRV mentions were wearable.
7. **The questionnaire/PRO filter over-excludes.** Of 30 sampled demo-cluster mentions that it flagged as non-objective (population 121), 23 were in fact objective uses sharing a sentence with a questionnaire (false-exclusion rate 0.77 (0.59-0.88)); 5 were correctly excluded. Because few mentions are flagged, the count impact is small; the largest demo-cluster change is `accelerometry` in me_cfs (17 -> 22 trials when flagged mentions are counted, `n_trials_any_counted_mention_sensitivity`). The filter is not a validated PRO classifier.
8. **Manual functional tests dominate over digital gait.** In Long COVID literal trials, outcome text names the 6-minute walk test in 100 trials and sit-to-stand in 48, whereas instrumented digital gait appears in 3. These tests are objective but they fall outside every measurement class, so the registry does not count them (`results/tables/measurement_manual_functional_tests.csv`).
9. **The condition assignment matters.** Summed over classes, the MeSH-expanded trial set gives 2.23x the literal-match count for dysautonomia and 10.43x for mcas (mostly multiple system atrophy / orthostatic hypotension and mastocytosis / leukaemia trials, per the ClinicalTrials.gov audit). Only the literal view is used for evidence. A trial linked to several conditions is counted in each condition's row, so the per-condition counts are not additive (demo-cluster unions are in the registry's `*_demo_cluster` columns).
10. **NIH grant text is noisier than trial text.** Most round-1 'no' judgements for transcriptomics, proteomics and immune assays were grant background sentences or animal work. The non-human context flag removed 271 otherwise-objective mentions from the evidence counts, but it only sees the mention's own sentence.
11. **Registration is not validation, and absence is not evidence.** A zero in `condition_measurement_evidence` means the miner found no mention. It does not mean the measurement is unused or unsuitable. Recall is not measured.

## 3. Precision audit

Design: for each class, a seeded random sample of 30 objective mentions (a census when fewer existed) from demo-cluster records in counted roles. Grants were de-duplicated to one fiscal year per core project. Each snippet was judged yes / no / unclear against the class scope in `configs/measurements.yaml`. Strict precision = yes / judged (unclear counts against). Classes below 0.8 were revised (now recorded per class under `audit_revision` in `configs/measurements.yaml`, the single source of the patterns; changes logged in `measurement_pattern_changelog.csv`) and re-audited on a **fresh** sample that excluded every round-1 mention. **All judging was done by an AI reviewer (a language model), not a domain expert, from the snippet alone.**

`status`: `meets_0_8` = strict precision >= 0.8 on >= 10 judged mentions from >= 5 distinct records; `insufficient_sample` = point estimate >= 0.8 on fewer; `below_0_8`; `design_data_only` = no fresh post-revision sample. The record-clustered interval uses the worst-case effective sample size (sum m)^2 / sum m^2 over records, so it is conservative. These two columns were added at review; the rule is stated here and in `precision_audit.py`.

## 8. Query layer

`measure_it.measurements.query.discover_candidate_measurements(condition, phenotype=None)` orders candidates by a transparent lexicographic rule:

   1. more registered trials for the condition with objective use of the class (ClinicalTrials.gov literal condition match)
   2. more of those trials register it as an outcome measure
   3. more NIH-funded core projects mentioning it (RePORTER title/abstract precision view)
   4. higher technology_maturity tier (established > emerging > category_only > no_fda_category)
   5. lower curated deployment_complexity (1 = home/consumer device ... 5 = specialist lab)
   6. measurement id (alphabetical) for stable ties

The order ranks research-deployment activity, not validity. `get_measurement_evidence(measurement, condition)` returns the evidence rows with trial:/nih:/fda:/geo_series: object_ids and snippets. It returns UNKNOWN / NOT AVAILABLE when the input does not resolve or when nothing was found. An exact class id wins over a bundle alias ('capillaroscopy' is the class; 'CAPRIO' is the bundle), and a phenotype word that fits several axes equally ('orthostatic', 'sleep') returns UNKNOWN with the candidates instead of a guess.

Example: `discover_candidate_measurements('Long COVID', phenotype='post-exertional malaise')` returns 1. cpet (53 trials), 2. accelerometry (39 trials), 3. wearable_heart_rate (14 trials), 4. ecg_ambulatory (7 trials).

## 9. Deviations from the analysis plan

1. A class-independent **non-human context flag** was added after round 1 (animal and cell work in grant text). It removes mentions from the evidence counts and was covered by the round-2 audit.
2. A **sentence-window bug** was fixed while judging round 2. RePORTER abstracts are hard-wrapped, so a single newline was being treated as a sentence boundary. The round-2 sample was not re-drawn. The summary reports precision re-scored on the judged mentions still in the population (`precision_strict_current_population`).
3. `quantitative sensory testing` was considered for small_fiber_testing and **rejected before round-2 sampling**, because its mentions were pain-threshold testing in fibromyalgia and migraine trials.
4. For `continuous_temperature`, no fresh post-revision mention existed, so the registry reports design-data precision and labels it as such.
5. The PRO filter was audited but **not** retuned after its false-exclusion rate was measured. Retuning it on the audited sample would have tuned on test data.
6. **Independent review (2026-09-23)** changed the following; no pattern or judgement was changed. (a) A precision status and a record-clustered interval were added, so classes resting on a handful of mentions are no longer reported as passing. (b) Samsung Medison (hospital ultrasound) was being counted as a consumer-device applicant (7 spurious vascular_imaging consumer clearances; now 0). (c) Filtered subsets of a product code that a bundle also maps whole were double counted in FDA decision totals. (d) Sensitivity columns for dysautonomia's broad 'autonomic dysfunction' links and for bundles without HRV. (e) Ratio intervals for the comparator. (f) The query layer returned guesses for ambiguous phenotype words ('orthostatic', 'intolerance', 'sleep') and resolved the class id 'capillaroscopy' to the bundle that includes microvascular_function; both now resolve as described in section 8 or return UNKNOWN. (g) Example object ids are now written deterministically.
