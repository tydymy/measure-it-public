### What this does and does not show

* **Wearable features alone discriminate worse than a 9-variable demographic model**: point estimate lower for 6 of 6 targets, 95% CI below 0 for 5 of 6 (CI includes 0 for mortality); Delta AUROC wearable-only minus demographics from -0.074 to -0.011. Accelerometry is not a stand-alone classifier of these self-reported phenotypes in this sample.
* **Test 1 (added to demographics): wearable features add signal for 5 of 6 targets** (functional_limitation, fatigue, fair_poor_health, depression_phq9, mortality; Delta AUROC +0.019 to +0.032, all 95% CIs > 0; wearable-only permutation p <= 0.05 for 5 of 5; Holm-adjusted bootstrap p <= 0.05 for 5 of 5, post hoc). Not for mecfs_like_proxy.
* **Test 2 (added to a full clinical model): the increment is small or absent.** Primary-model 95% CI > 0 for functional_limitation, fatigue, depression_phq9 (Delta AUROC +0.0069, +0.0046, +0.0057); CI includes 0 for fair_poor_health, mecfs_like_proxy, mortality. CI > 0 in all three model families AND in the 2011-12 -> 2013-14 split: functional_limitation. Holm-adjusted across the 6 targets (post hoc): functional_limitation 0.012, fatigue 0.200, depression_phq9 0.170; only functional_limitation survive(s). The other CI > 0 results should be read as unconfirmed.
* Positive control (mortality): wearable-only AUROC 0.845 (0.830 to 0.860), but much of that is age (demographics alone 0.856 (0.844 to 0.869)); the accelerometry-specific evidence that the pipeline sees the known signal is the increment over demographics (+0.0193 (+0.0131 to +0.0254)). Over the clinical model the AUROC increment is null (+0.0016 (-0.0012 to +0.0046)) while AUPRC (+0.0193 (+0.0081 to +0.0304)) and Brier (-0.0012 (-0.0018 to -0.0005)) improve slightly; the overall ranking (AUROC) is essentially unchanged.
* **ME/CFS-like proxy (PROXY, not ME/CFS): a measurable group difference, no predictive increment.** Wearable-only AUROC 0.582 (0.536 to 0.627) is above its permutation null (null max 0.581, p = 0.005), but adding wearable features to demographics (+0.0139 (-0.0126 to +0.0387)) or to the clinical model (+0.0172 (-0.0037 to +0.0376)) is not supported. The adjusted signature has 14 of 26 features passing FDR. Top FDR features (SD units, age/sex-adjusted): sleep_proxy_midpoint_sd_h +0.33 (excl. depression +0.37, excl. arthritis +0.25); interdaily_stability -0.32 (excl. depression -0.42, excl. arthritis -0.29); active_period_duration_h -0.32 (excl. depression -0.26, excl. arthritis -0.29). In the sensitivity variants the effect sizes are of similar size, but with 71 and 81 cases only 1 and 9 features pass FDR: the sensitivity analyses are underpowered and neither confirm nor rule out that the signature is carried by co-occurring depressive symptoms or arthritis.
* **No discrete wearable subgroups.** HDBSCAN labels every participant noise (also under 4 other min_cluster_size/min_samples settings: none finds a cluster); GMM BIC keeps improving up to k = 8 (boundary) and its partitions are only moderately reproducible (see §10). Cluster-target associations describe regions of a continuous activity-pattern distribution.
* Prescription reason-code groups (2013-14 only; signatures only): fibromyalgia n = 34: 13/26 FDR features; migraine n = 69: 0/26 FDR features; ibs n = 12: 0/26 FDR features; insomnia n = 146: 19/26 FDR features; myalgia n = 90: 10/26 FDR features. Migraine and IBS are null; the fibromyalgia signature (34 cases) is defined by taking a drug and may reflect the medication as much as the condition.
* Nothing here involves heart rate, HRV or autonomic physiology (none exists in NHANES 2011-2014), so these results say nothing about wearable autonomic monitoring.

## 13. Limitations

* Cross-sectional: accelerometry and questionnaire targets were collected in the same survey wave; associations have no temporal direction (mortality is the only prospective outcome).
* Self-reported labels: functional limitation, fatigue (one PHQ-9 item), self-rated health and PHQ-9 are questionnaire measures, not clinical diagnoses. Prescription reason codes identify treated people in one cycle (2013-2014), with tiny case counts (IBS 12, fibromyalgia 34).
* The ME/CFS-like proxy lacks the defining features of ME/CFS (post-exertional malaise, duration, clinical exclusion work-up); exclusions rely on self-report and the ever-told cancer item.
* No heart rate, HRV or any autonomic signal exists in NHANES 2011-2014: the wearable module here is wrist accelerometry only (MIMS, single activity cut-point, algorithm sleep proxy). Nothing here evaluates autonomic monitoring.
* Survey design (weights, strata, PSUs) is not used for prediction metrics: every AUROC/AUPRC/Brier describes this unweighted wearable-valid sample, not the U.S. adult population; the valid-wear subset is not re-weighted for non-wear selection.
* Temporal split: 2013-2014 participants have about two years less mortality follow-up than 2011-2012, so prevalence and calibration-in-the-large shift by design for that outcome.
* Model-level findings are group-level discrimination; none of this supports statements about an individual participant.

## 14. Deviations from the analysis plan

Copied verbatim from §10 of `docs/ANALYSIS_PLAN_NHANES.md`:

_(appended during/after the analysis; nothing above this section was edited after the analysis started)_

1. **Metrics stage re-run (no refit).** The first full run finished the cv, temporal, perm and shuffle stages
   and then crashed at the start of the metrics stage: joblib could not unpickle scipy's `logit` ufunc
   sent from `__main__`. `logit`/`expit` were replaced by mathematically identical numpy functions (unit tests
   compare the calibration fit with statsmodels to 1e-6), the entry point now runs through the importable
   module, and only the metrics stage was re-run on the cached out-of-fold predictions. No model was refitted
   and no specification changed.
2. **HDBSCAN stability reporting.** HDBSCAN labelled every participant as noise (0 clusters). The ARI between
   two all-noise labelings is trivially 1.0, so the stability code was changed to record ARI as not
   estimable (NaN) in that case and to report the number of clusters per bootstrap replicate. The
   unsupervised run was stopped and repeated with this change; PCA/GMM/UMAP use fixed seeds and are
   unaffected. The HDBSCAN bootstrap refits on the unique resampled participants (duplicated points
   distort density estimates), consistent with §8.
3. **Descriptive additions not in the plan** (none changes a pre-specified estimate or decision rule):
   a robustness qualifier in the results headline (agreement of the Test-2 CI across the three model
   families and the temporal split); a `context_clinical_vs_demographics` Delta-AUROC; a table describing
   the composition of the ME/CFS-like proxy and what each exclusion diagnosis removed; coefficients of the
   primary model refit on the whole population (descriptive only); an explicit caveat that Rx reason-code
   signatures may reflect the medication itself.
4. **Resolution of the shuffle control.** As planned, 20 shuffles were run; the smallest attainable
   empirical p is 1/21 = 0.048, so a "passing" shuffle control means that the observed increment exceeded
   every shuffled one, not that p is small. Stated in the results.
5. **Independent review (2026-09-23; no estimate changed).** The full pipeline was re-run end to end: every
   out-of-fold prediction, permutation and shuffle draw, and every `nhanes_*.csv` result table was
   reproduced byte for byte (only `nhanes_run_metadata.csv` timestamps differ). Changes made by the reviewer:
   (a) *Query fixes* in `signatures.get_patient_phenotype_signature`: text matching now uses whole tokens of

   reference contrast); model-level AUROC rows stored by other producers are reported under
   `model_level_results` instead of as top wearable features (previously the only "top feature" for Long

   results; a missing `null_value` is reported as UNKNOWN instead of being assumed to be 0.
