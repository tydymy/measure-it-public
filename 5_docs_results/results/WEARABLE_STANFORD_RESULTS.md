## Headline results (positive, negative and null)

* **Test 1, Long COVID (Uwakwe 2025, n = 126, 31 cases): NULL RESULT for the wearable features.** Whole-record resting-HR features alone gave AUROC 0.504 (95% CI 0.404 to 0.605; label-permutation p = 0.300) with the primary L2-logistic model. The demographic/clinical baseline (age bin, sex, BMI, race, vaccination) gave AUROC 0.702 (0.598 to 0.796; p < 0.001).
* **Test 2, multimodal increment: NULL RESULT.** Adding the wearable features to demographics changed AUROC by -0.024 (95% CI -0.076 to 0.027; wearable-block permutation p = 0.469); demographics+wearable AUROC 0.677.
* Univariate wearable features (Test 1, secondary): 1 of 7 have a pre-specified permutation p < 0.05 (cusum_alarms_per_30d: Hedges' g 0.46, -0.03 to 0.97, p = 0.027, studentized-permutation p = 0.091); 0 survive BH correction; 0 of 7 have p < 0.05 under a studentized (Welch) permutation test, which stays valid when the smaller Long COVID group is more variable. No single whole-record resting-HR feature is a robust Long COVID signal here.
* Leakage-labelled reference only: the 7 symptom items of the paper's symptom model reach AUROC 0.857 (0.764 to 0.934). The label is defined by persistent symptoms, so this is not a comparator for a measurement; it shows how much of the published discrimination is carried by self-report.
* **Negative control changes the reading of 'acute RHR elevation'.** In the 84 placebo-eligible COVID-19-positive participants, 62% had a day with RHR z > 2 in days -7..+14 of the true onset, but so did 56% of their own pre-infection placebo windows (paired difference 6.3 percentage points, 95% CI -5.3 to 19.7; p = 0.332). The single-day elevation flag is not specific to infection in these data. Placebo onsets have short baselines more often than true onsets (enumerated from the start of each record); restricting both to baselines of >= 20 RHR days gives the same answer (difference 6.0 percentage points, -8.0 to 20.4; p = 0.414; n = 70).
* **Resting HR rises briefly around onset; steps fall for longer.** Mean RHR z peaks on day 0 at 1.03 (95% CI 0.63 to 1.44) versus a placebo band of 0.01 to 0.13, and its lower CI is back inside the placebo band from day 2. The pre-specified 0..14-day mean RHR z therefore does not separate true from placebo onsets (paired difference 0.11 SD, 95% CI -0.20 to 0.45; p = 0.531: a null result for that endpoint), whereas mean step z over days 0..14 is -1.05 SD (paired difference vs placebo -1.10, 95% CI -1.33 to -0.88; p < 0.001).
* Recovery (among 68 participants with an acute elevation; 52 recovered, 16 censored): Kaplan-Meier median 6 days from onset to 3 consecutive in-band days (95% CI 3 to 9). This is a descriptive trajectory, not an infection-specific recovery time: measured from the acute peak with follow-up capped at +14, true onsets return to the baseline band in a median 5 days (3 to 8; n = 52) and the same people's placebo 'elevations' in 6 days (5 to 7; all 2958 elevated placebo onsets of 78 participants, participant-weighted); one random placebo onset per participant gives 8 days (5 to 11). Measured from the reference day the placebo median is longer (8 days participant-weighted, 10 for one random onset, vs 6 for true onsets), but that reflects where the peak falls (median peak day 4.0 for placebo vs 0.5 for true onsets), not a different recovery after infection.
* **Onset-vs-placebo window discrimination (participant-grouped CV):** days 0..+7 AUROC 0.861 (95% CI 0.792 to 0.917; label-swap p < 0.001; n = 74 participants); the model-free mean RHR z alone gives 0.531 (0.439 to 0.625; p = 0.267). Pre-symptomatic days -7..-1: model 0.562 (0.473 to 0.646; p = 0.115), mean RHR z 0.558 (0.466 to 0.646; p = 0.095). This is retrospective window discrimination with a known onset date, not prospective early warning.
* The days 0..+7 model is carried mainly by the step drop: descriptive univariate AUROC of mean step z 0.131 (i.e. 0.869 with lower steps scored as onset), versus 0.618 (0.532 to 0.706) for max RHR z and 0.531 for mean RHR z (descriptive, untested). Reduced walking in the week after a known COVID-19 onset is expected behaviour, so this is a measurable phenotype of acute illness, not evidence of a latent physiological signal. Wear-time check (added in review): relative to the same people's placebo windows, the share of calendar days that are valid wear days falls by 7.3 percentage points after onset (95% CI 2.0 to 12.7), so some ill days drop out of the analysis altogether, but on the valid wear days that are analysed HR minutes per day change by 6 (-12 to 25). The step drop on analysed days is reduced walking, not shorter wear.
* Descriptive, no placebo comparator (added after the plan): over days 15..90 after onset, mean RHR z is 0.38 (95% CI 0.16 to 0.64; n = 87) and mean step z -0.32 (-0.53 to -0.09). Placebo windows cannot extend past +14, and the placebo RHR z already drifts to 0.13 by placebo day +14 as the baseline ages, so this post-acute RHR offset cannot be separated from baseline drift with these data.
* Temporal-leakage and consistency checks: 0 of 8 checks with violations (table below).

## A. Uwakwe 2025 Long COVID discrimination (Test 1 / Test 2 on an invisible-illness label)

Cohort: 126 participants, 31 Long COVID / 95 without (female 23/31 vs 42/95; vaccinated before infection 21/31 vs 81/95). Label = self-reported symptoms >= 12 weeks after a confirmed COVID-19 diagnosis (not a clinical diagnosis). Wearable features are whole-record resting-HR summaries of the authors' step-masked HR; the release has no diagnosis date, so nothing is aligned to infection, and no steps are released. Validation: 10x5 repeated stratified CV; 2000 participant-bootstrap resamples; 1000 label permutations per model (each re-running the full CV). Missing values imputed in-fold (counts: bmi 9, rhr_night_mean_record 4, cusum_alarms_per_30d 2).

## Negative and null results (stated plainly)

* Whole-record resting-HR features do not discriminate self-reported Long COVID in Uwakwe 2025 (L2 AUROC 0.504, CI 0.404 to 0.605, p = 0.300).
* No multimodal increment over the demographic/clinical baseline (delta AUROC -0.024, CI -0.076 to 0.027, block-permutation p = 0.469).
* No model family rescues the wearable-only set: AUROC logistic 0.523 (p = 0.374), l2_logistic 0.504 (p = 0.300), random_forest 0.557 (p = 0.186).
* acute RHR elevation fraction (peak z > 2 in days -7..+14): true vs placebo onset paired difference 0.063 (CI -0.053 to 0.197, p = 0.332) — not distinguishable from placebo.
* mean RHR z, days 0..14: true vs placebo onset paired difference 0.114 (CI -0.203 to 0.451, p = 0.531) — not distinguishable from placebo.
* No single whole-record resting-HR feature separates the groups robustly: 0 of 7 pass BH correction, and 0 of 7 have p < 0.05 under the studentized permutation test (the one nominal pre-specified result, cusum_alarms_per_30d, does not survive it).
* Recovery time is not infection-specific: timed from the acute peak, 5 days (3 to 8) after true onsets vs 6 days (5 to 7) after the same people's placebo 'elevations'.
* Detection window A (days 0..7), model-free: mean RHR z in window: AUROC 0.531 (CI 0.439 to 0.625, p = 0.267).
* Detection window B (days -7..-1), L2 logistic (C=1) on mean z, max z, mean step z: AUROC 0.562 (CI 0.473 to 0.646, p = 0.115).
* Detection window B (days -7..-1), model-free: mean RHR z in window: AUROC 0.558 (CI 0.466 to 0.646, p = 0.095).

## Limitations

* Uwakwe 2025: n = 126 (31 cases); self-reported label with recall bias; age only in 10-year bins; comorbidities missing for 84/126 (not used); per-participant device not released; HR already step-masked by the authors; no steps; no infection date, so every wearable feature is whole-record and cannot address the paper's post-diagnosis window.
* Acute cohorts: highly engaged smartwatch volunteers; source dates are shifted per person (only within-person intervals are meaningful); participants may stop wearing when ill; Apple Watch (HealthKit) HR is sparse and noisier; Alavi participants without a published positive are not verified negatives and are not used; 17 of the 107 analysed reference days are test dates without a reported symptom onset.
* Placebo eligibility needs >= ~50 days of pre-infection data, so the placebo-eligible subset over-represents participants with long records (Alavi) and under-represents Mishra.
* Only Mishra 2020 records other illness episodes, so only Mishra placebo windows can exclude them. An Alavi placebo window may contain an unrecorded non-COVID illness, which would push the negative control toward the null for the resting-HR endpoints. The mean placebo step z stays near 0, which suggests such windows are uncommon.
* Bootstrap CIs describe evaluation-sample uncertainty for fixed CV predictions; they do not include model-refit variability (shown separately as the range over CV repeats).
* Nothing here is linked to any geographic or facility row; these participants have no location data.

## Deviations from the analysis plan

* Detection table: added descriptive univariate AUROCs of each of the three pre-specified window features (no fitting, no test), to show which feature carries the window-A model. Added after the first run showed the model and the model-free RHR score disagree; used only to describe which feature carries the model, never as a test.
* Endpoints table: added DESCRIPTIVE mean RHR z and mean step z over days 15..90 for true onsets only (no placebo comparator is possible because placebo windows end at +14). Added after the trajectory plot showed RHR z above the placebo band in weeks 3-5; not tested, not written to the signature table.
* Detection windows: the one random placebo window per participant is drawn among that participant's eligible placebo onsets that also have >= 3 observed RHR-z days in the feature window, and only complete true/placebo pairs are analysed (so the within-participant label-swap null is well defined). The plan said 'one random eligible placebo window' and '>= 3 observed days' without fixing the order of the two rules.

### Post-review changes (independent review, 2026-09-23)

* Signature table: the demographic-only and demographics+wearable model AUROC rows were removed. The signature query lists every row with q < 0.05 as a top feature, so the demographic baseline was being returned as the only 'feature' of the Long COVID wearable signature. Their values are quoted as context in `caveats`. Uwakwe BH q-values are therefore computed over two fewer rows. Added the columns the query reads: feature_label, effect_measure, phenotype_definition, is_proxy, caveats.
* Recovery time: added Kaplan-Meier rows timed from the acute peak (true vs placebo, follow-up capped at +14), a participant-weighted KM over every elevated placebo onset, and the peak-day distribution. The earlier write-up compared medians timed from the reference day (placebo longer than true) and read it as evidence about recovery; that contrast reflects where the peak falls, not recovery speed.
* Univariate Uwakwe features: added a studentized (Welch-t) permutation p over the same permutations (the pre-specified raw mean-difference permutation test is anti-conservative when the smaller group is more variable).
* Negative control sensitivity: the elevation fraction and 0..14-day mean RHR z are repeated with true and placebo baselines of at least 20 RHR days (placebo onsets near the record start have short baselines more often).
* Wear-time check (descriptive): change in the share of valid wear days and in HR minutes per valid wear day, days 0..14 vs own baseline, true minus placebo. Tests whether the step drop is reduced walking or reduced wearing.
* km_summary drops (and counts, column n_dropped_no_followup) rows with a missing recovery time/event instead of casting NaN to an integer, which would have corrupted the Kaplan-Meier input had an elevated window had no follow-up day.
