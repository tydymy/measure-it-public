# Published lab and biomarker evidence for invisible illnesses

**Every number on this page is a published claim — not reproduced by this project.** Each number is copied from a verbatim quote stored with its row in `data/processed/published_lab_evidence.parquet` (109 claims from 102 publications, curated 2026-09-24). The build re-fetches every abstract or open-access full text from Europe PMC and checks every quote word for word. On the last build, all 109 quotes were verified. Every AUROC, sensitivity and specificity value, and every count marked "as stated", was found inside its row's quote. Every DOI matched Europe PMC. Rows are cited as `PLE-###`. The object id is `published_lab_evidence:PLE-###`. The module is `measure_it.labs.published_lab_evidence` (query: `get_published_lab_evidence(condition=None, biomarker=None)`). The audit and search log are in `data/raw/published_lab_evidence/DATA_AUDIT.md`. The companion device table is `docs/PUBLISHED_DEVICE_EVIDENCE.md`.

This is a targeted curation, not a systematic review. A study missing from the table is not evidence that none exists. Nothing here concerns a participant in any dataset this project processed. These are condition-level published results: layer 2, "condition-level molecular evidence" in `docs/SPEC.md`.

## The short answer

Lyme serology is an established test for the infection, though its accuracy depends on the stage of disease and it cannot identify PTLDS. Apart from it, no lab test for the twelve target conditions has validated, externally replicated diagnostic accuracy against symptomatic look-alikes. The strongest findings, condition by condition:

| condition | strongest published lab finding | why it is not a diagnostic test |
|---|---|---|
| MCAS | The **tryptase 20% + 2 ng/mL** rule and urinary mediators (LTE4, 2,3-dinor-11β-PGF2α / 11β-PGF2α, N-methylhistamine) *are* the consensus criteria (PLE-001, PLE-002). Three patterns run through the table:

* **Most rows compare patients with healthy people.** 46 rows use healthy comparators only, 19 use symptomatic or disease comparators and 23 use both. Where a study measured the difference (Lyme serology, PLE-091), specificity fell from about 95% against healthy controls to about 80% against symptomatic people.
* **Positive discovery results often fail to replicate independently:** POTS autoantibodies (PLE-031/032 vs PLE-033), Long COVID cortisol (PLE-040 vs PLE-042/043), migraine CGRP (PLE-098 vs PLE-099), IBS antibodies (PLE-101 vs PLE-104/105), endometriosis salivary miRNA (PLE-115 vs PLE-116) and serum miRNA (PLE-117 vs PLE-118).
* **Incorporation bias is structural for MCAS and hyperadrenergic POTS.** The biomarker is part of the definition, so "accuracy" there means agreement with the rule (18 rows flagged yes or partial).

## How to read a row

| column | meaning |
|---|---|
| `claim_type` | Only `diagnostic_accuracy` says anything about classifying a person. `group_difference` and `prevalence_in_cases` describe groups. `defining_criterion` means the biomarker is part of the definition. `no_validated_biomarker` means the source states that none exists. `treatment_response_prediction`, `prognostic_association` and `passive_transfer_experiment` (mechanistic) are the other types. |
| `incorporation_bias` | `yes` means the biomarker is part of the diagnostic criteria or reference standard used. `partial` means it is part of it for some patients or stages. |
| `comparator_type` | `healthy`, `disease_or_symptomatic` (look-alikes), `mixed` (both) or `none` (cases only). |
| `external_validation` | `yes` only when accuracy was checked in an independent cohort. A held-out split of the same sample is not external validation (PLE-058, PLE-117). |
| `evidence_tier` | `pooled` (meta-analysis or systematic review), `large_primary` (about 100 cases or more), `small_primary`, or `definition`. |
| `source_quote` | Verbatim quote segments. Every number in the row comes from here. |

**What the tests are.** The 2012 consensus criteria (PLE-001) require three things: typical symptoms; a substantial transient rise in serum tryptase or in other mast-cell mediators or their urinary metabolites; and a response to mediator-targeting drugs. The tryptase rise is operationalised as **acute ≥ 120% of baseline + 2 ng/mL** (PLE-002). These tests are therefore *defining* criteria, and no study can estimate their sensitivity for MCAS against an independent reference (incorporation bias).

**What is known about their performance.**
* **Urinary mediators.** The only sensitivity and specificity figures found are for **systemic mastocytosis**, in 66 SM cases among 409 referrals: LTE4 48% / 84% (PLE-004), 11β-PGF2α 53% sensitive (PLE-005), N-methylhistamine 71% (PLE-006), and the panel of all three 97% (PLE-007), with specificity not quantified for the latter three. In MCAS case series without controls, 11β-PGF2α was the mediator most often raised (PLE-008, PLE-009). Within tryptase-confirmed episodes in mastocytosis, urine mediators rose several-fold, with an LTE4 ratio of 35.98 (PLE-010). These episodes were selected by tryptase.
* **The 20% + 2 rule has a false-positive rate from baseline variation.** Without any reaction, 9.7% (9/93) of people met it, and more in HαT (18%) and indolent SM (30%) (PLE-003). The authors propose an acute/baseline ratio of 1.685 instead, chosen in-sample.
* **Suspected MCAS is rarely confirmed.** In a prospective real-life cohort of 100 patients referred with suspected MCAS, a tryptase rise was seen in 2 of 79 and MCAS was confirmed in 2% (PLE-011). A mastocytosis centre confirmed idiopathic MCAS in 4.4% of 703 (PLE-012). This is the ascertainment problem in `docs/DATA_ACCESS_PLAN.md` ("Lab biomarkers in All of Us").
* **KIT D816V in blood** separates clonal from non-clonal mast-cell disease: 94% of 83 SM detected (PLE-013); 90% of 48 SM, with 5 missed, prospectively (PLE-014). By definition it says nothing about idiopathic MCAS.
* **Baseline tryptase and hereditary α-tryptasemia (HαT).** Extra TPSAB1 copies occur in about 5% of unselected people (PLE-016) and raise baseline tryptase. The normal range is 1–15 ng/mL (PLE-019). Genotype-specific reference ranges change the interpretation (PLE-020).

**MCAS vs look-alikes (POTS, EDS/HSD, IBS).** The original HαT report linked TPSAB1 duplications to IBS, dysautonomia and connective-tissue features in symptom-selected families (PLE-015). Later work did **not** confirm the connective-tissue link: HαT was not increased in 266 people with hypermobility (PLE-017). A 2025 systematic review found no study meeting its criteria for an MCAS–HαT–POTS–EDS relationship (PLE-018). The POTS–mast-cell link goes back to a small subgroup defined by raised urine methylhistamine (PLE-021). In IBS, mucosal mast cells are raised on average (colonic SMD 0.38–1.69, PLE-022; ileum 1.78 but duodenum and jejunum not significant, PLE-023). But GI biopsy mast-cell counts do not separate MCAS from inflammatory look-alikes (PLE-024). **No study scores a mast-cell mediator against POTS, EDS or IBS comparators with an independent reference.** The All of Us design in `docs/DATA_ACCESS_PLAN.md` is built to describe that gap, not to close it.

* **Upright norepinephrine.** "Hyperadrenergic POTS" is *defined* by standing NE ≥600 pg/mL (or a blood-pressure rise), which gave 27 of 300 patients in PLE-025. A 2026 series printed "161 (41%)" of 223, which is internally inconsistent (161/223 is 72%), and symptom burden did not differ by NE phenotype (PLE-026). The sampling time on tilt changed the label in 1 of 19 (PLE-028). In children, orthostatic NE predicted metoprolol response with AUC 0.785 (76.9% / 91.7%, n = 25, in-sample; the cut-off unit is printed as "pg/ml", PLE-027). No study reports how many healthy people exceed 600 pg/mL upright.
* **Adrenergic / muscarinic GPCR autoantibodies.** Positive in small studies: α1 in 89% of 55 by ELISA (PLE-031); AUC 0.88 for a cell-based activation assay, 48 vs 25 (PLE-032). **Null in the largest study**: 116 POTS vs 81 controls, with α1 "seropositive" in 98.3% of POTS and 100% of controls at the vendor threshold (PLE-033). Also null in a 2026 study with a PASC comparator (PLE-034). Among Long COVID patients, removing the antibodies by immunoadsorption did not help (PLE-052, sham-controlled).
* **Blood volume.** Plasma volume deficit 334 ± 187 vs 10 ± 250 mL, with aldosterone 190 vs 380 pmol/L and no renin difference (15 POTS vs 14 controls, PLE-029). Blood volume by CO-rebreathing 64.53 vs 76.78 mL/kg, correlating with upright HR at r = −0.608 (PLE-030). Consistent, but small and against healthy controls.
* The POTS diagnosis itself is a device measurement (HR on standing). See `docs/PUBLISHED_DEVICE_EVIDENCE.md`, PDE-001.

* **Cortisol.** Klein 2023 reported lower cortisol, with AUC 0.96 for cortisol alone and 0.94 for a multidimensional immune classifier, against convalescent and healthy controls (PLE-040, PLE-041). * **RECOVER (Erlandson 2024).** Among 1,880 people with PASC index ≥12 and 3,351 with index 0, none of 25 routine labs differed meaningfully or was a clinically useful PASC biomarker (PLE-047). This is the strongest null in the table.

* **GPCR autoantibodies** (PLE-069). A β2 difference appears in ME/CFS only, at p = 0.046. The antibody-removal RCT was null (PLE-052).

* **Anti-CdtB / anti-vinculin** (post-infectious IBS hypothesis). Developer studies report high specificity and low sensitivity against IBD: anti-CdtB 91.6% / 43.7%, AUC 0.81 (PLE-101); a second-generation assay 93.5% / 43.0% (PLE-102). The test is weak for IBS-C (26.7% vs 16.3% in healthy controls, PLE-103). **Both independent replications are null**: a population sample plus outpatients (PLE-104), and IBS-D 43.2% vs controls 45.7% positive (PLE-105).
* **Faecal calprotectin** is the useful lab test in IBS-like symptoms, but as a *rule-out* of IBD: 85.8% / 91.7%, with NPV 99.8% at 1% prevalence (PLE-106). It excludes a look-alike. It does not detect IBS.
* Mucosal mast cells in IBS: see the MCAS section (PLE-022, PLE-023, PLE-024).

## What this table is not

* It is not a systematic review, and it has no risk-of-bias scoring tool (QUADAS-2 was not applied by us). The risk-of-bias notes are the curator's reading of each abstract.
* It is not reproduced. `project_reproduction` is "not attempted" for every row. No public dataset processed by this project has these lab values on people with the target labels.
* It does not score or rank biomarkers into a single number. Evidence tier, comparator, incorporation bias and external validation are shown side by side, as `docs/SPEC.md` requires.
